Scream canistercontrols

A set of buttons and controls I built from the scream canisters in Monsters, Inc.

I wanted every state to come from something the canister actually does in the film. The look comes from the Monsters, Inc. title cards, and the timing comes from going through the films frame by frame.

Why a scream canister?

In the movie, a scream canister fills up, seals, overloads and gets tagged out. Buttons do all of that too, so I gave each state a canister mechanic.

Sight glass
Progress. Seven cells fill in half a second.
Check valve
Busy. Extra clicks during a fill get ignored, and the cog jams.
Overpressure
Error. The valve blows and a red pen circles the cause. The same button retries.
Lockout tag
Disabled. A danger tag says why the button is locked.
Hold to seal
Confirm. Hold for a second to lock the cap.
Venting
Undo. The canister drains, faster than it filled.

Made the way the films were made

I went through the Monsters, Inc. and Monsters University title sequences one frame at a time.

Motion curves measured from the movies

Instead of grabbing stock easing curves, I measured mine from the films. I tracked 24 moves across the title cards and the Monsters University opening credits. One of them is a logo landing. It squashes from 33 px to 24 px over three frames.

  • Frame 1, 1.20 sThe logo lands, 33 px tall.
  • Frame 2, 1.24 sSquashed to 27 px.
  • Frame 3, 1.28 sFully squashed at 24 px, held for 2 frames.

The button press uses the curve I fit through those frames. Its first drawing lands at .661, right where the logo sits on frame 2.

All 24 moves came down to 12 curves: press, release, snap, arrive, rise, drop, glide, run, overshoot, overshoot-soft, settle and whip.

What surprised me is how gentle the curves in the films are. The punch comes from the timing. Squashes hold for two frames, launches take one, and stops are sudden, so the buttons copy the timing too.

Timed in drawings

I counted every move in drawings at 24 frames per second. A press is two drawings (83 ms), a release is three (125 ms), and a fill takes the film's half second.

Title lettering from the title cards

Letters drop in one at a time, and one always shows up late, just like the title cards.

An exposure sheet for buttons

The more often you see a moment, the shorter I made it. Hover has no delay. A seal, which you only see now and then, gets a full twist-lock.

Drawn from the real prop

I drew the canister from the official Disney Parks canister and checked it against film stills, down to the flat top, the wider cap and foot, the recessed bands and the seven-cell window. Seven cells of 1,200 SUV add up to the 8,400 SUV on the prop.

Flat cut paper

The title cards are flat paper shapes with no outlines, so the canisters, loader and titles follow that rule too. I hand cut the buttons at first, but smooth pills worked better as controls, so the paper look stayed with the illustrations.

Accessibility

  • Every control has a focus ring, in every browser.
  • Disabled buttons stay reachable by keyboard and tell you why they're locked.
  • With reduced motion on, nothing moves, and state still shows through color.
  • Loops stop when they scroll off screen.

Developer handoff

The handoff has the tokens, a state matrix, all 12 curves as CSS, timing for every move and an accessibility checklist. The motion is plain CSS with no animation library.
